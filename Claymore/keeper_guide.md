# Verloren
Welcome, new keeper! This document provides guidance on running games on the Verloren campaign's discord server.

# The Verloren Campaign
This campaign is built around game sessions 2-4 hours in length for a party of 2-5 adventurers. Games take place in a discord voice chat with Owlbear Rodeo serving as a virtual tabletop, and use the Claymore TTRPG rules. The table is "open", which means that the party may vary between game sessions. This is also being structured to allow for multiple keepers to run games.

## Game Formats
Here's the different sorts of games run on the server.

### Job of the Week(JOTW)
These are small, self-contained adventures that last a single session. The simplest plot for a JOTW might be a literal monster hunt, but mysteries, heists, and five room dungeons are also options.

### Plot Arc
Multiple sessions with separate B plots, but a shared A plot. One format is to have three sessions with a three act structure, making the third a climax that might be a boss battle or big confrontation.

### Player Request
Players request a session to complete some objective, and the scenario is prepped to make it happen.

### Expedition
A fixed party embarks on a multi-session adventure.

### Onboarding Session
In this campaign, players play through the backstory of their characters in a first session. In groups of 3-4, a funnel can be used where each player gets multiple commoners, and picks one survivor to be their character. With only one or two new players, a much shorter backstory session can be run, pulling in any interested established players to control NPCs.


# Class Guide
Here's some meta info about how the classes are set up.

## Martials
In general, no special handling is needed. The Brute, Ranger, Brawler, Knight, and Commander just fight really well, so throwing them a chance to show off their combat abilities doesn't hurt. For the stealthy classes such as Thief, Ranger, and Brawler, varied terrain will make bypassing combat, sneak attacks, and sniping or overwatch more viable. Adding even one piece of cover to battle map transforms it from a flat killbox to something that can be potentially stealthed through. Verticality, such as trees, boulders, roofs, and balconies is also great. I'll do a per-class breakdown below.
- Thief: Stealthy scout and skill monkey
- Brute: Melee Tank
- Ranger: Stealthy glass cannon
- Commander: non-magical necromancer that brings minions
- Brawler: Melee striker, effective HP buffed by using stances and stealth
- Knight: Capable of reliable nova damage, especially against supernatural targets.


## Wizard
Wizards scale geometrically, and exiting their larval stage offers powerful area of effect and reality-bending spells. Might want to give the spells list a once-over to make sure you don't have any puzzles that are insta-solved by them.

## Witch
Healbots that can revive the party with magic. Give them an opportunity to interact with animals or nature spirits here and there. If they try to befriend animals, have them make a few contested animal handling checks(advantage if they use food). Four or five should be needed to tame an animal, with every failure resulting in either hostility or the critter fleeing and needing to be caught again. If anyone is to tame a wild creature, it's a witch.

### Alchemy Check DC guide
To make a potion, an alchemist consumes one or more ingredient and makes an alchemy check to brew it.
Brewing happens in a lull in the action, so you can afford to take your time reasoning through an answer. Even still, you will not be held to any decisions made. A potion's difficulty may change from brew to brew. That's just alchemy for you. Use these guidelines only if they help you.
The base DC for that alchemy check is 5. Here are some guidelines for common modifiers.
Consuming multiple ingredients that correspond to the same effect decreases the DC:
- Two ingredients: -2 DC
- Three ingredients: -4 DC
- Four ingredients: -6 DC
Damage-dealing effects increase the DC as follows:
- 2 Damage: +1 DC
- D6 Damage: +2 DC
- 2d6 Damage: +4 DC
- 3d6 Damage: +8 DC
- 4d6 Damage: +13 DC
- 5d6 Damage: +16 DC
Healing potions increase the DC as follows:
- 2HP: +4 DC
- D6HP: +6 DC
- 2d6HP: +10 DC
- 3d6HP: +13 DC
- 4d6HP: +15 DC
- 5d6HP: +18 DC

### Common Potion Guidelines
A poison can be dipped ten times. If the entire potion is injected or consumed, it deals maximum damage.
A poison with a special effect(like paralysis or igniting the target) has double duration or effect when consumed.
Most beneficial potions can't be dipped for a reduced effect(Add +5 to the DC for an exception).

## Priest
A priest's magic relies upon creating spells that correspond to names with cost in spirit points negotiated.
If you want a vague guideline for the cost of damage dealt, 2 damage costs 0 spirit points, and every D6 of damage costs 1(ie 5d6 = 5 spirit points). This should allow them access to a huge, but unsustainable spike of damage. With a few increases in spirit points, 15d6 damage is possible, albeit at the cost of emptying a priest's power.
